# Housing Price vs. Supply 2024

Stephanie Armstrong, Ilgaz Kuscu, Alexander D. Silberman, Baylee Wechsler

## Housing Price vs. Supply 2024 EDA

We were interested in exploring the relationship between housing prices and supply, to test how well the economic model of supply and demand maps to this real-world example. To that end, our SMART research question is as follows: how is the supply of housing related to average home prices across U.S. metropolitan areas in the year 2024? To answer this question, we will analyze data from the American Enterprise Institute Housing Center’s State and County Housing Market Indicators: Update for 2024 dataset, found [here](https://www.aei.org/the-state-of-the-housing-market/), specifically looking at the 2024 median housing prices and the number of months it would take for the inventory of homes for sale to be exhausted at the current sales pace. Our dataset will include 9241 metropolitan-area-level observations, giving us a broad picture of how housing supply and prices vary across the country.

## Housing Price vs. Supply 2024 Modeling

In the first project, we examined the relationship between months of supply and median home prices across U.S. metro areas in 2024 using data from the American Enterprise Institute (AEI).

While the analysis identified a clear association between months of supply and median home prices, further exploratory analysis revealed that months of supply is not a raw measure but rather reflects the pace of inventory absorption. This limited our ability to fully understand housing price dynamics and motivated us to seek out new datasets.

The second project uses raw supply measures while incorporating socioeconomic context. The analysis relies on the CBSA-level 2024 Realtor.com dataset as the core housing market dataset. This dataset is enriched with 2024 one-year estimates from the American Community Survey (ACS) to capture demographic, economic, and housing characteristics across U.S. metros.

From the 2024 ACS 1-Year Estimates Data Profiles, we selected variables that provide an overview of population composition, income, and housing characteristics for each CBSA included in the Realtor.com data.

By integrating housing market indicators with structural socioeconomic variables, this project aims to better distinguish whether observed housing price patterns in 2024 are primarily driven by supply constraints, demand-side pressures, or underlying demographic and economic conditions across U.S. metropolitan areas.
